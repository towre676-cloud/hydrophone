Deciding well with incomplete information

A fusion of material memory, musical constraints, process accounting, measurement design, and independently checkable decisions.

Built on recovered 2023–2026 conversations and the existing DEFORM workbench. The executable results below are mathematical models. This package acquires no physical trials and establishes no physical speedup.

The organizing question is practical: what is the least we must measure before an action is justified? A material can conceal its loading history, a musical target can cancel before reaching the tuning controls, and a cooking process can change both nutrient amount and its denominator. Those are different physical problems. They can nevertheless share an exact decision layer: retain all states compatible with the evidence, calculate which actions meet the task in each state, and gather only the additional observations needed to find an action that works throughout the remaining uncertainty.

The October 22 DEFORM report already supplies morphology-dependent dynamics, energy accounting, operator closure, and a certified two-memory order example. This work builds on that existing architecture. Its concrete addition is a small executable engine that distinguishes full reconstruction, task identification, and sufficient information for action; includes closed bounded-noise intervals; and exposes incompatibility witnesses involving more than two possible states.

The shared mathematical contract

Let θ denote a possible state, history, calibration, or parameter configuration. Each application supplies its own physically meaningful model. A clay adapter may use internal deformation and acoustic ring-down; a music adapter may use a spelled interval Jacobian; a process adapter may use quantities retained, transferred, and lost. The common engine does not pretend these variables have the same units. It receives a finite declared hypothesis population Θ, a candidate action universe A, a loss or success criterion, and the possible readings of each candidate probe.

Gθ = {u ∈ A : action u meets the declared task under θ}
Θy = {θ ∈ Θ : observation y is possible under θ}
Choose u ∈ ⋂θ∈Θy Gθ.

That intersection is the decision certificate. It may be nonempty even when the internal state cannot be identified. It may also be empty after the task is identified, because the available controls cannot accomplish the task. The engine must keep these possibilities separate. A task tolerance, an observation error bound, and an actuator restriction are different quantities.

More generally, with continuous controls and a loss ℓ, define E(y) = infu∈U supθ∈Θy ℓ(θ,u). A declared tolerance τ is met when E(y) ≤ τ. For the finite action implementation, this condition is checked exactly through the sets Gθ. The continuous expression describes the extension; this package does not claim to implement a general nonlinear robust optimizer.

Why checking every pair can fail

Take three task types whose acceptable candidate actions are G₁={a,b}, G₂={b,c}, and G₃={a,c}. Every pair shares an action: types 1 and 2 can use b, types 2 and 3 can use c, and types 1 and 3 can use a. All three together share none. Therefore an algorithm that asks only which pairs need distinguishing can conclude that no measurements are required and still fail to justify any action.

This is a geometric phenomenon as well. Use targets t₁=(0,0), t₂=(2,0), and t₃=(1,2), candidate controls a=(1/2,1), b=(1,0), and c=(3/2,1), and accept squared distance at most 5/4. Direct rational calculation gives the loss rows (5/4,1,13/4), (13/4,1,5/4), and (5/4,4,5/4). These produce exactly the three action sets above.

The obstruction persists if every point in the plane is allowed as a control. The best control without observations is the circumcenter (1,3/4), with worst squared loss 25/16, exceeding 5/4. One exact certificate uses positive weights 5/16,5/16,3/8 on the targets: their weighted average is that circumcenter, so the weighted average squared distance from any control equals its squared distance from the circumcenter plus 25/16. The maximum loss is at least that average. At the circumcenter, all three distances equal 25/16, proving the minimax value. With only the three listed candidate controls, the no-observation minimax value is instead 13/4.

Compatible collections of hypotheses form a simplicial complex: a collection is included exactly when its action sets have a common point. This is the nerve of the action sets. The example has all three edges but lacks the filled triangle. Its minimal missing face is the three-way conflict that a pairwise graph loses. This language is useful because our older geometric and topological instincts now identify an operational question: which measurements eliminate precisely those unresolved collections that prevent action?

An exact obstruction-cover criterion

Call S an obstruction when the intersection of its acceptable-action sets is empty, and call it minimal when removing any hypothesis makes the intersection nonempty. Suppose probe p under hypothesis θ can return any value in the nonempty closed interval Ipθ=[lpθ,upθ]. For the following exact criterion, a hypothesis may independently produce any combination of values in its intervals across the chosen probes. Let P be a fixed probe panel.

P permits a robust observation-to-action policy
⇔ for every minimal obstruction S, some p ∈ P satisfies
maxθ∈S lpθ > minθ∈S upθ.

Proof. If the inequality fails for every selected probe, the intervals of all hypotheses in S have a common point in every coordinate. Their product supplies one observation compatible with the entire obstruction. No action is acceptable for all of it. Conversely, if an observation leaves no common action, its finite compatible population contains a minimal obstruction. That observation belongs to every interval in the obstruction, so no selected coordinate can satisfy the strict inequality. This contradicts the stated condition.

The strict inequality matters. If intervals meet at one endpoint, that endpoint is an allowed observation. With nonnegative additive probe costs cp, minimizing total cost is an exact weighted hitting-set problem: each minimal obstruction must be excluded by at least one selected probe. The supplied demonstrator enumerates all panels, so its stated minimum is an exhaustive optimum for its finite model. Exponential enumeration is a transparent baseline, not a scalable algorithm for large archives or instruments.

Small failure witnesses follow from the action space. With m finite candidate actions, every minimal obstruction contains at most m hypotheses. For each member of a minimal obstruction, there is an action accepted by every other member but rejected by that member. These witness actions must be distinct. The bound is sharp for Gᵢ=A∖{aᵢ}. For a finite family of convex continuous action sets in d dimensions, Helly's theorem gives the standard bound d+1 instead. Nonconvex actions and infinite populations need their own hypotheses; the finite convex result cannot silently be applied to them.

The six-state experiment and its noise boundary

Duplicate each of the three task types with a nuisance bit n∈{0,1}, giving six hypotheses. The decision probe returns 1 for type 3 and 0 otherwise, at cost 1. The task probe returns 1 for type 2 and 0 otherwise, at cost 2. The nuisance probe returns n, at cost 4. Each observation has independent closed error interval ±ε.

For ε<1/2, one decision probe suffices. Its low response identifies types 1 or 2, for which b is acceptable; its high response identifies type 3, for which a is acceptable. Identifying the exact type requires both the decision and task probes, costing 3. Identifying the nuisance bit as well requires all probes, costing 7. There are eight minimal action obstructions, each a triple containing one hypothesis of every type. Every pair of the six hypotheses is compatible.

At ε=1/2, every binary probe can return 1/2 under either nominal output. The transcript consisting entirely of 1/2 is compatible with all six hypotheses, so no panel can guarantee an acceptable action. This is an exact boundary for the declared independent adversarial-noise model. Repeating the same probe does not defeat such a worst-case bound. A statistical averaging guarantee would require an additional probabilistic noise model.

The Python program checks all eight panels at noise radii 0,1/10,1/2 and performs another 576 panel checks on seeded random finite models. Its hyperedge criterion agrees on all 600 cases with a separate closed-interval arrangement enumeration. The latter visits interval endpoints and one point in every open gap, forms all joint compatibility cells, and checks their common actions directly. These checks validate the implementation on the tested instances; the theorem's guarantee comes from the proof.

Memory, observation, and actuation remain separate

A second exact example uses state x=(q,m₁,m₂,n), where q is a visible coordinate, m₁ and m₂ are hidden memories, and n is nuisance. The task is a=m₁+2m₂. Observe q before and after a modeled probe that changes it to q+a while leaving the memory coordinates fixed. The observation matrix has rows (1,0,0,0) and (1,1,2,0). Its rank is only two in a four-dimensional state space, yet their difference identifies a exactly. Hidden directions (0,−2,1,0) and (0,0,0,1) remain unobservable and irrelevant to this task.

For controlled residual r=(a,a)+(1,2)u, knowing a gives optimal u=−3a/5 and squared residual a²/5. The witness w=(2,−1) is orthogonal to the actuator direction (1,2): its component cannot be changed by that actuator. If a is initially unknown in [−1,1], robust u=0 has worst squared loss 2. Adding actuator direction (1,−1) allows exact cancellation with controls (−2a/3,−a/3). These are exact model calculations. They show why a measurement improvement alone cannot prove that a physical target is reachable.

In a physical material, a probe may itself alter the memory. Its potential responses must then be derived from common-reset trials or treated as complete history-dependent protocols whose final acceptable-action sets are recomputed. The fixed-panel theorem assumes fixed potential responses and fixed success sets. It does not license combining destructive measurements as if they were passive independent sensors.

The music connection is an exact reachable-space obstruction

The saved Dyletsky engine constructs 36 directed interval rows for major thirds, minor thirds, and fifths over twelve pitch classes. Fixing the reference pitch gives an 11-column Jacobian J. The supplied replay reconstructs that integer matrix and checks det(JᵀJ)=52,002,816 and rank 11. Targets and weights constant within each interval family give JᵀWb=0 exactly. Thus a ridge solution x=(JᵀWJ+λI)⁻¹JᵀWb remains zero even when target residual is nonzero.

This is a concrete instance of edge requests whose achievable part is determined by vertex adjustments. Graph Hodge theory supplies an established mathematical language for gradient-reachable edge data and cyclic inconsistency. Applying that language to the pitch engine is a musical interpretation of existing theory. In the fusion, it supplies a reachable-action check before any optimizer is credited with success. Changing priorities uniformly across a whole interval family preserves the cancellation; the encoded note-dependent weights can break that symmetry.

How recovered subjects enter one engine

Clay and acoustic instruments supply history-dependent hypotheses, calibrated input-output operators, and probes that may change the state. The practical target is to select a useful diagnostic excitation and a justified adjustment while retaining uncertainty about hidden material history. The existing DEFORM fading-memory and order certificates provide model-level starting points; calibration must establish their physical applicability.

Music and chant supply incompatible interval targets, restricted pitch controls, spelling and context dependence, and audible outcomes. A common acceptable action may exist across several interpretations of a phrase even when its exact latent style is unresolved. Historical authenticity and listener preference require their own evidence; a least-squares proxy cannot establish them by itself.

Cooking and process accounting supply quantities retained, transferred, and lost across operations. Raw concentration, final concentration, total retained amount, and physiological delivery remain separate variables. A proposed adapter could retain several process hypotheses and select a preparation that meets a declared accounting criterion across them. This package supplies no food-specific kinetic estimates, absorption predictions, or dietary recommendations.

Manufacturing and geometry supply feasible-action restrictions: contour error, acceleration, jerk, actuator range, and timing. For a circle at constant speed, acceleration is v²/R and jerk is v³/R². Consequently, a visually acceptable geometric change may still fall outside the machine's feasible action set. A timing change and a geometry change should be represented as distinct controls.

Downloads, traces, and archive recovery supply completeness and identity requirements. A checker must reject an empty intended population rather than treat universal validity over zero records as coverage. A trace hash can bind recorded bytes; it cannot establish an unrecorded laboratory event or prove the mathematical correctness of the recorded result. The included engine rejects an empty hypothesis population and keeps its synthetic status explicit.

Prior work and the contribution actually made here

Functional observability already studies recovering relevant functions without reconstructing all hidden state. Decision Region Determination already studies overlapping regions of hypotheses that support the same decision; Javdani and colleagues developed a hyperedge-cutting framework in 2014. Noisy testing and adaptive decision procedures also have substantial prior literature. Helly's finite convex obstruction bound is classical. These precedents prevent treating this synthesis as a newly discovered universal theory.

The deliverable is a concrete integration: an exact bounded-interval implementation, independently checked observation cells, geometric and actuator obstruction examples, a replay of the archived music matrix, and an explicit bridge from recovered physical concepts to decision contracts. A research advance would require a genuinely stronger algorithm or theorem, or measured savings against relevant decision-region and functional-observer comparators. The illustrative costs 1,3,7 are consequences of this example's declared probe menu, not evidence of sevenfold industrial savings.

A useful next experiment would freeze a real application, enumerate or bound its plausible histories, derive acceptable actions from a calibrated model, and compare the cheapest robust decision panel with a full-reconstruction panel and a strong existing decision-oriented method. Total cost must include model identification, calibration, resets, computation, failed probes, and final outcome verification. Correlated noise, drift, omitted hypotheses, or unmodeled probe disturbance can invalidate a guarantee and must enter the model rather than be hidden behind a certificate.

Replay and evidence

The package contains fusion_engine.py, results.json, this report, and README.txt. Python uses only the standard library, with exact fractions and integer elimination. The interactive illustration uses ordinary browser arithmetic; the executable proof checks are authoritative for the supplied example.
