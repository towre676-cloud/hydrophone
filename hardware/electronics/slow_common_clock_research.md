# Common-clock slow force, optical displacement and marker candidate

> **Research history.** [E01 README.md](README.md) and [channel_plan.json](channel_plan.json) govern current candidate wiring and settings. The released candidate uses 5 Hz DC-coupled force/laser filtering and the M03 600 × 360 mm base.

**Earlier 3 kg alternatives superseded.**

Primary manufacturer material retrieved **24 October 2026, America/New_York**. This is a candidate wiring and qualification plan, not an assembled or calibrated logger. No price was verified and nothing was purchased. It complements the common-clock audio interface; the audio and slow ADC clocks remain separate and require measured marker alignment.

The practical shared-slow-clock baseline is **FUTEK LSB200 FSH00103 + LabJack T7 + LJTick-InAmp 3, factory gain 201 + Micro-Epsilon ILD1420-10CL1 with PCF1420-3/U cable**. The selected raw full-bridge cell is nominally 5 lb / 22.2 N, 2 mV/V and 350 ohms; connect it by its documented excitation/signal functions. Its mounting, side-load and bidirectional calibration limits still apply. The earlier 3 kg Phidgets alternative is not the baseline. The force and displacement voltages and an actual electrical synchronization marker are streamed by the same T7. This avoids treating two unrelated USB callback clocks as one sample clock, and the laser does not load the moving carrier like a contact indicator. See the [manufacturer LSB200 drawing, FSH00103 capacity row](https://media.futek.com/content/futek/files/pdf/productdrawings/fsh00103.pdf).

## Verified manufacturer facts

LabJack's bridge application note explicitly supports raw bridge measurement using T7 hardware. The current InAmp 3 supplies two channels, selectable factory gains of 11, 51 or 201 and a fixed 1.25 V output offset. Its internal 2.5 V reference is rated to source 20 mA. InAmp output is `1.25 V + gain*(IN+ - IN-)`; loading must respect its output range. The current product is **InAmp 3**: the older InAmp 2 is discontinued. See [bridge application note](https://support.labjack.com/docs/bridge-circuits-app-note) and [InAmp 3 datasheet](https://support.labjack.com/docs/ljtick-inamp-3-0-datasheet).

The T7 analog inputs offer four nominal ranges, ±10, ±1, ±0.1 and ±0.01 V. However, **±0.01 V internal amplification is not supported for multichannel streaming** in the current stream table. Do not configure a raw bridge at that range and simultaneously promise synchronized displacement/marker streams. External InAmp conditioning permits all streamed T7 channels to use ±10 V. At resolution index 7, the manufacturer's four-channel maximum is 315 scans/s; **the initial baseline is 50 scans/s**, with 200 scans/s an optional later configuration to qualify separately. Samples within a scan are sequential: the gain-1/index-7 interchannel delay is approximately 670 microseconds, not zero. See [ranges](https://support.labjack.com/docs/14-3-0-analog-inputs-t7-t-series-datasheet), [stream table](https://support.labjack.com/docs/a-1-1-stream-data-rates-t-series-datasheet), and [input specifications](https://support.labjack.com/docs/a-3-2-1-t7-ain-general-specs-t-series-datasheet).

The current Micro-Epsilon family datasheet specifies **ILD1420-10CL1** with a 10 mm measurement range, 20–30 mm stand-off, ±8 micrometre linearity and 0.5 micrometre repeatability under the stated reference-surface/processing conditions. CL1 is laser class 1 and has a maximum 4 kHz measurement rate. The general 1420 family uses 11–30 VDC, below 2 W at 24 V; the `PCF1420-3/U` option yields a 1–5 V signal. **CL1 analog conversion is 12 bit**, although the ordinary class-2 model's current family datasheet advertises 16 bit. This difference must appear in the purchase specification and uncertainty budget. See [current family datasheet, pages 6–7](https://www.micro-epsilon.com/fileadmin/download/products/dat--optoNCDT-1220-1320-1420--en.pdf).

The ILD1420 manual identifies supply pin 7/red, ground pin 12/blue, laser-enable pin 8/black and analog-output pin 11/white. Connect pin 8 to pin 12 to enable the laser. The `/U` cable includes the voltage-conversion components; adding another 250-ohm shunt would change the transfer and must not be done casually. These details refer specifically to **ILD1420**, not a similarly named thicknessSENSOR. See [manufacturer manual, installation section](https://www.micro-epsilon.com/fileadmin/download/manuals/man--optoNCDT-1420--en.pdf). Full PDF retrieval timed out in this session; the manufacturer's indexed installation passages supplied these pin assignments. Confirm the supplied cable's current pin sheet before wiring.

## Proposed exact connection map

| Connection | Destination | Configuration and meaning |
|---|---|---|
| T7 VS/GND | InAmp 3 VS/GND through a labelled breakout | Only power/ground pass straight through. Do not plug the Tick directly across AIN0/AIN1 when inserting an external filter. Use printed net labels, not an assumed physical pin order. |
| InAmp OUTA, isolated from the T7 input pin | Force filter CH1 input; filter CH1 output to T7 AIN0 | No direct OUTA-to-AIN0 connection may bypass or short the filter. Isolate OUTB from AIN1; it is not streamed. |
| InAmp VREF 2.5 V / GND | Load-cell EX+ / EX− | A 350-ohm bridge draws a calculated 7.14 mA, below the stated 20 mA reference limit. Verify the actual cell permits this excitation. |
| Load-cell SIG+ / SIG− | InAmp INA+ / INA− | Use the load-cell manufacturer's wiring, not assumed wire colours. AIN0 is the conditioned force channel. |
| Unused InAmp INB+ and INB− | InAmp GND | Do not leave unused amplifier inputs floating; AIN1 is not needed in the scan list. |
| Dedicated 24 V instrument supply + / 0 V | ILD1420 pin 7/red / pin 12/blue | Supply is for measurement electronics, separate from actuator power. Connect signal ground to T7 GND at the planned reference point. |
| ILD1420 pin 8/black | Pin 12/blue | Enables the laser. Preserve the class-1 model and the supplied manufacturer's instructions. |
| PCF1420-3/U pin 11/white voltage signal / blue ground | AIN2 / T7 GND, through the displacement low-pass channel | Nominal 1–5 V represents the configured measuring span, not automatically displacement from the experiment's datum. |
| T7 DAC0 actual marker node / GND | AIN4 / GND | Read back the real marker voltage. Generation command time is not the observed event time. |
| Same marker node, conditioned for audio | Dedicated audio line input | The electronics owner specifies the divider, protection, grounding and any isolation. Suggested node amplitude is 1 V; do not route 24 V to audio. |
| InAmp VREF / GND | AIN6 / GND | Optional reference-voltage monitor, useful for ratiometric checks. |

Use scan list `[AIN0, AIN2, AIN4, AIN6]`, all single-ended against GND, all range ±10 V, `STREAM_RESOLUTION_INDEX=7`, initial `ScanRate=50`. Record the actual accepted scan rate, scan list, stream counters/drop indications and per-channel offsets within each scan. Four simultaneous values cannot be assumed. Save raw voltages as well as calibrated force/displacement. USB arrival times do not define these sample times. The optional 200-scan/s variant must not silently change the timing assumptions of the initial plan.

Place the stationary optical head about 25 mm from a diffuse target fixed to the **moving clay plate/carrier near the clay deformation plane**, measuring along the shear coordinate. The sensor head stays on the fixed structure, adding no moving sensor mass. The target and sensor mounting must expose carrier pitch or account for it; one optical point cannot by itself separate translation and rotation. Calibrate the actual target surface, datum, orientation, thermal drift and local 0.5 mm operating stroke rather than copying full-span nominal accuracy into a certificate.

## Filtering, marker and resolution limits

The T7 high-speed ADC is broadband, so a 50 Hz scan does **not** itself provide a 25 Hz anti-alias filter. For the intended 0.015–2 Hz slow experiment, put a real DC-coupled analog low-pass filter in the force and laser paths before their ADC inputs. A concrete laboratory candidate is a **Krohn-Hite 3364**, two channels at a proposed 5 Hz cutoff, Butterworth low-pass, DC coupling and unity input/output gain; its manufacturer specifies four independent 4-pole channels and ±10 V input at unity gain. The currently linked datasheet gives a stopband specification above 80 dB; use that conservative value rather than the different 100 dB figure in an older manual. It is not an 80 dB rejection guarantee at every frequency above Nyquist: the ideal four-pole curve at 25 Hz with a 5 Hz cutoff is about 55.9 dB down, and actual alias rejection and delay require end-to-end measurement. A 10 Hz cutoff would provide only about 31.8 dB ideal attenuation at the 25 Hz Nyquist boundary. The 5 Hz cutoff has substantial phase delay in the experiment band, which must be measured and included in alignment/calibration. It is external bench equipment, not an enclosure-sized assumed part; obtain an actual quote. See [currently linked manufacturer datasheet](https://krohn-hite.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/3364Data-1.pdf) and [current model page](https://krohn-hite.com/model-3364-24db-octave-slope/). A smaller replacement may be selected only with equivalent verified DC/passband/stopband and delay requirements. Keep the synchronization readback unfiltered or explicitly characterize its filter delay. The slow-writing protocol should disable acoustic drive; a sampled slow force channel is not a dynamic force transducer for the musical experiment.

Measure the force and laser filter group delays separately, including their buffers and cables. The unfiltered marker aligns clock axes; it does not remove either sensor's filter delay. For quasistatic hold comparisons, wait for measured settling and record that interval. For strokes or hysteresis, retain per-channel transfer/delay corrections and their uncertainty; a common nominal cutoff is not proof of matched delay.

Generate identifiable marker bursts before and after the experiment, with plateaux at least 100 ms, and read their actual edges in both ADC records. At the initial 50 scans/s, one slow-sample interval is **20 ms**; allow that interval in marker picking unless a smaller interpolation bound has actually been verified. Add filter/path delay and interchannel skew rather than assuming the interval is the full uncertainty. Measure the residual clock-map error. The optional 200-scan/s variant has a 5 ms interval, but neither variant promises sub-sample synchronization. Fit offset and drift from several corresponding edges. Retain the raw marker series and detector thresholds. A fixed audio path's high-pass coupling can turn a plateau into edge transients, so identify the same physical leading edge rather than equating arbitrary peaks.

For the **selected FUTEK LSB200 FSH00103 nominal 22.2 N, 2 mV/V bridge**, 2.5 V excitation gives 5 mV at full scale; gain 201 gives `1.005/22.2 = 0.04527 V/N` at the ADC. A 0.01 N change is therefore approximately **0.453 mV**. The nominal full-negative-range output is only 0.245 V, close to the InAmp's lower output limit; cell zero balance and amplifier offset can consume that headroom. The initial **±5 N** envelope gives nominal output **1.024–1.476 V**, before offset/gain errors, and must be calibrated in both load directions. The T7's nominal ±10 V 16-bit step is about 0.305 mV, and averaging/resolution index can reduce noise but cannot remove systematic calibration error. The manufacturer's ±2 mV absolute ±10 V specification alone corresponds to roughly **0.0442 N** with this nominal gain. Accordingly **0.01 N absolute force accuracy is not established**; use end-to-end loaded calibration and an independently measured uncertainty budget. Similarly, a 10 mm/12-bit laser span is a calculated 2.44 micrometre per analog code before ADC, optical linearity, target and thermal effects. Neither code size nor 0.5 micrometre repeatability certifies 10 micrometre absolute displacement accuracy.

The purchased head, InAmp, load cell and filter each need model/serial/configuration records. Store actual transfer coefficients, excitation voltage, electrical zero, sign and load direction; retain force and displacement baselines. All physical conversion remains unqualified until those records and bounds have been independently checked. This candidate removes contact-indicator loading and shares the slow sampling clock, but it does not establish a cheap complete metrology system or new physical results.
