PASSIVE TWO-MODE INSTRUMENT MODEL
Date: 2026-10-24. All numbers are illustrative, assumed SI coefficients.

MODEL AND ENERGY
Let q=(q1,q2) be generalized displacements, v=qdot, y=a^T q, and z the relaxed internal displacement of a Maxwell branch. The dynamics are M qdd + C qdot + K0 q + a[k_inf y+k_m(y-z)] = B u, and zdot=(y-z)/tau. With positive M,K0,C,k_inf,k_m,tau, stored energy is E=1/2 v^T M v+1/2 q^T K0 q+1/2 k_inf y^2+1/2 k_m(y-z)^2. Differentiating gives Edot=u B^T v-v^T C v-(k_m/tau)(y-z)^2. Thus this precisely specified model is passive, although a passive model is not proof the assembled apparatus is passive or that any active feedback is stable.

If k_inf and k_m change with time, add P_write=1/2 k_inf_dot y^2+1/2 k_m_dot(y-z)^2 to Edot. This is work done by reconfiguration. One cannot claim the same unforced decreasing-energy theorem while changing the material parameters. If a, K0 or M also change, their corresponding differentiated energy terms must be included. The slow plunger, string-tuning hardware and active driver need measured force/displacement or electrical power to account for their work.

COMPLEX RESPONSE
At angular frequency omega, the clay dynamic stiffness is k*(omega)=k_inf+k_m i omega tau/(1+i omega tau). The two-mode mechanical frequency response is H_mech=(K0-omega^2 M+i omega C+k*(omega)aa^T)^(-1)B. The included synthetic sensor sees gain times the first mechanical coordinate; this gain is explicitly frequency independent and real. The water cell and hydrophone do not have this known conversion without calibration.

A LIMIT THAT CHANGES THE EXPERIMENT
At fixed parameters the incremental frequency response is independent of initial z. Two different histories may have different relaxation offsets, but small acoustic sweeps yield the same incremental linear response. The SLS state is fading memory, not nonvolatile plastic shape storage. Detecting a written clay configuration acoustically requires that history change a measurable coefficient, geometry, contact, equilibrium state with state-dependent stiffness, or coupling; otherwise the proposed readout is blind. This is a counterexample to an unqualified claim that any clay memory will be audible.

There is a second exact nonidentifiability. If the hydrophone/water transfer G_water(omega) is completely unknown, measured response H_obs=G_water H_mech cannot identify H_mech: for any nonzero candidate H_mech_new choose G_water_new=H_obs/H_mech_new. A displacement/velocity reference and controlled calibration are therefore essential. With pressure-only sensing, use empirical signature comparison under fixed setup and explicitly avoid claiming unique material-parameter recovery.

SIMULATION AND SCOPE
The script uses two passive mechanical coordinates, an assumed tau of 2 seconds, one known drive distribution, and one synthetic sensor with unknown constant gain. It computes local relative sensitivity singular values, fits 30 noisy trials at 1% independent Gaussian noise in each complex response component, and numerically integrates free and forced energy balance. Slow frequencies from 0.015 to 2 Hz augment an acoustic sweep from 65 to 240 Hz. Low frequencies belong to a force/displacement experiment, not an audible or ordinary hydrophone measurement. All parameter-recovery results condition on this correct model and noise law; no empirical confidence, hardware sensitivity, material lifetime, or speedup is established.

The program writes reproducible model_results.json, synthetic_sweep.csv, free_ringdown.csv and model_diagnostics.png beside itself. Install numpy scipy matplotlib if required; run python passive_instrument_model.py. No actual hydrophone, amplifier, or mechanical fixture is driven. No repository API has yet been integrated.

NUMERICAL RESULTS (exactly reproducible with the declared seed)
Analytic complex-transfer derivatives and SVD covariance give sensitivity condition number 1.4629978698e8 for the 65-240 Hz audio-only sweep, versus 207.8849148 for the 0.015-2 Hz slow plus 65-240 Hz sweep. A sixth nuisance parameter, an independent unknown constant gain for the slow sensor, changes the combined condition to 208.6650881; its local tau log-error is 0.01825185 rather than 0.01606093. Both cases continue to assume frequency-independent gains and a correct known forcing distribution. Actual force/displacement channel calibration and actual water-path calibration remain separate required measurements.

Do not interpret the enormous audio-only local covariance as a meaningful global confidence interval. It diagnoses an almost unobservable parameter combination. Finite-difference estimates are also numerically fragile: step sizes 1e-4, 1e-5 and 1e-6 produce audio condition estimates about 1.13e6, 1.09e8 and 1.47e8, respectively. The main reported condition uses analytic derivatives; the finite-difference sensitivity results are retained in JSON to expose that failure. Combined-sweep conditions agree to roughly 0.0012 over those same step sizes.

Thirty independent 1%-noise fits with the original shared-gain assumption gave relative RMSE 3.7727% for k_inf, 0.5962% for k_memory, 1.4727% for tau, 0.1431% for gain and 1.7126% for damping scale. This Monte Carlo result is conditional on the declared illustrative model, not measured material performance. The separately parameterized gains case is a local sensitivity calculation only, not a second Monte Carlo experiment.

The free-response energy-balance residual was 1.95834e-18 J; forced balance residual 5.75718e-22 J. Free energy fell from 6.219426e-5 to 6.575978e-6 J over 0.5 s; every sampled energy increment was negative. Identical replays were bit-for-bit identical in this runtime. Independent five-state frequency-domain elimination agreed with the dynamic-stiffness law to a maximum relative error 5.8323e-16 at six checked frequencies.

Changing the assumed k_inf by +20% and k_memory by -20% left a 47.1844% complex transfer-shape residual after fitting the best single complex gain. This establishes only that this particular parameter change is distinguishable from a constant gain in this model. It does not establish that a clay write produces those parameter changes. The illustration's modal frequency region differs from any proposed musical string targets; it must be refitted after those masses, tensions, bridge conditions and fluid loads are measured.
